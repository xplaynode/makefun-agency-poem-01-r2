# Tournament — agency-poem-01 round 2b

Single-elimination pairwise tournament over all 16 candidates in
candidates.md (C01–C16). Every match is judged with the kit judge rubric,
in priority order:

1. It is a poem AND its subject is choosing/acting, or having, handing
   over or taking back the power to act.
2. It moves the reader — an image, turn or ending that lands and stays.
3. Insight — something true about agency, recognised at once, never a
   slogan or a lecture.
4. Craft — concrete, exact words, none that could be cut; sound, rhythm
   and line breaks serving the meaning.
5. Freshness — its own voice and images, not stock phrases.

Each match below gives a one-line reason citing the first criterion that
separated the pair (unless a later criterion differed by much more).

## Round of 16

- M1: C01 beats C16 — Criterion 2: the interpreter pausing so the choice stays the defendant's lands harder than the ceremony's gentler return of the pen.
- M2: C08 beats C09 — Criterion 2: stepping past her own stand-in to say yes herself is the sharper turn; the dictated letter stays at one emotional pitch.
- M3: C05 beats C12 — Criterion 4: the sealed ballot and bought noodles are concrete and uncuttable, while the agent poem explains its lesson in prose-like lines.
- M4: C07 beats C14 — Criterion 2: the shaky signature witnessed in silence out-moves the lunch menu's polite, lower-stakes hand-back.
- M5: C03 beats C10 — Criterion 2: the receiver taken with both hands, "like something hot," stays longer than the contract clause crossed out.
- M6: C06 beats C11 — Criterion 1: the lyric is only loosely about acting for another, while reading aloud is delegated agency enacted scene by scene.
- M7: C02 beats C15 — Criterion 3: "He's slow, not gone" names the line between help and erasure; the stand-in's insight is stated rather than discovered.
- M8: C04 beats C13 — Criterion 4: the saw, pencil line and un-fixed cut are exact and cut-free, while the second-person consent form leans on summary in its last line.

## Quarter-finals

- M9: C01 beats C08 — Criterion 3: translating a refusal so the choosing stays another's says more about agency than the borrowed "no," which resolves too neatly.
- M10: C07 beats C05 — Criterion 2: nineteen days of signing, then four minutes against four seconds, builds a feeling the single-errand proxy ballot cannot match.
- M11: C03 beats C06 — Criterion 3: grief's phone handed back shows agency returning as readiness, a finer insight than sight simply being restored.
- M12: C02 beats C04 — Criterion 2: waiting for a man to be wheeled from dialysis to sign for himself lands harder than the apprentice's earned first cut.

## Semi-finals

- M13: C07 beats C01 — Criterion 2: the mother reclaiming the clipboard with her good hand, witnessed and not overwritten, moves further than the courtroom's admirable restraint.
- M14: C02 beats C03 — Criterion 4: every detail — rent, Tuesdays, the unsigned credit offer — earns its place, while the phone poem has two lines that could be cut.

## Final

- M15: C07 beats C02 — Criterion 2: both are fully on-subject with real insight, but the rehab ward's final shaky name, out of its box and taking the whole line, is the ending that stays longest.

## Result

Winner: C07 — the frozen poem (the rehab-ward signature poem).
Winner's record: 4–0 (beat C14, C05, C01, and C02), undefeated.
Runner-up: C02. Semi-finalists: C01, C03.
